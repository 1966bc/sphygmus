HOW TO MEASURE BLOOD PRESSURE AT HOME

Your doctor's instructions come first. What follows is what the European
Society of Hypertension recommends for measuring at home; where your doctor
told you otherwise, do as your doctor said.


THE DEVICE

  - An automatic device with a cuff on the upper arm, clinically
    validated. Wrist devices are not generally recommended.
  - A cuff of the right size for your arm, used as the manufacturer says.
  - A device more than four years old is more likely to be inaccurate.


BEFORE MEASURING

  - Sit quietly for five minutes.
  - Back supported, feet flat on the floor, legs not crossed, the arm
    resting on a table with the cuff at the level of the heart.
  - Do not talk, during the measurement or between measurements.


THE MEASUREMENT

  - Two readings, one minute apart.
  - In the morning and in the evening; if you take medicines for blood
    pressure, before taking them.
  - Before a visit, for seven days, and not fewer than three, preferably
    ordinary working days.
  - Over the long term, once or twice a week or a month, as your doctor
    advises: measuring every day is discouraged.
  - Record every reading, as the device shows it. Do not leave out the
    ones you do not like.


READING THE NUMBERS

  - What counts is the average of the readings, not a single one.
  - At home the threshold is 135/85 mmHg, lower than the 140/90 used in
    the surgery. This diary marks the readings that reach it; it does not
    diagnose. Your doctor reads the diary.
